What the market actually ships

I looked at who wins today and, more usefully, what people complain about. The pattern is consistent: the platforms that are pleasant to use are priced for Western budgets, and the platform that is affordable everywhere (Moodle) is the one everyone describes as dated and painful. Nobody serves a Dhaka madrasa and a Dubai training company from the same product.

Two things fall out of this table. First, the market is split down the middle: creator platforms (EzyCourse, Tutor LMS) have no academic spine, and school systems (ClassTune, Ilmify, Pathshala) have no learning platform. Every institution that wants both is currently buying both, or buying neither. Second, the local tools understand the operational reality — Aliya/Qawmi dual streams, BEFAQ and BMEB boards, Hifz progress, bKash, Bangla — and the international ones understand none of it, while being ten to fifty times the price.

That space between them is Fajr. Not "a better Moodle" and not "EzyCourse with more features" — the one platform where a madrasa runs its term and sells a public course, with the local operational vocabulary built in rather than bolted on.

Why Fajr, and not the other one

Ten wedges. Each is something a named competitor structurally cannot or will not match — not a feature they simply haven't built yet.

01

Three tenant modes, one account

A madrasa runs internal classes and sells a public Tajweed course. A solo tutor grows into a school without migrating. Tenant type flips the module set; the core stays identical.

vs. Thinkific (no institution) and Canvas (no storefront) — you'd need both, and they don't talk.
02

Bidirectional text that actually renders

No translation layer — the interface is English. What matters is that content in Arabic, Bangla or Urdu renders correctly wherever it appears: a Gulf tenant authoring right-to-left, a Bangla lesson title inside an English admin table, an Arabic student name in a notification. See the section below; it is cheap now and near-impossible later.

vs. everyone international, where RTL is a theme setting and mixed-direction content scrambles.
03

Payments that actually clear locally

bKash, Nagad, SSLCommerz, JazzCash, Tap, PayTabs, Mada, Stripe — behind one interface. Plus fee installment plans and manual bank-transfer approval: parent uploads a deposit slip, admin approves, enrollment unlocks. That last one is how most South Asian school fees are genuinely paid.

vs. Kajabi / Thinkific / Docebo — Stripe or nothing.
04

Designed for a 3G phone on a village edge tower

Budget: under 100 KB on first paint. Audio-only track per video lesson, transcript-only fallback, downloadable lesson packs, 144p rung in every ladder. Ship a data-usage counter — students on prepaid data will love you for it.

vs. every Western LMS, which assumes campus wifi.
05

Offline is a feature, not a failure state

PWA and Expo app both cache lessons and queue quiz answers, assignment submissions and progress; sync on reconnect with server-side conflict rules. A student finishes a quiz on the bus.

vs. LearnDash / Kajabi, where "no offline mode" is a documented gap.
06

WhatsApp and SMS as the primary channel

Email is not how these markets communicate. Attendance alerts, fee reminders, grade releases and class links go to WhatsApp Business API and SMS, in the parent's language, with delivery receipts.

vs. everyone, who assume an inbox and get single-digit open rates.
07

Islamic education as a first-class module

Optional, switched on per tenant: Hifz tracking with sabaq / sabqi / manzil cycles, Qur'an progress by juz, surah and ayah, teacher listening logs, Tajweed error tagging, Hijri calendar, prayer-time-aware timetabling, and exam structures for the Aliya, Qawmi, BEFAQ and BMEB boards.

vs. Canvas and Docebo, which will never build it, and Ilmify / Muntazim, which have it inside a weak learning platform.
08

AI at the entry tier, not the enterprise tier

Generate a quiz from lesson content, auto-transcribe and translate video into Bangla, Urdu and Arabic subtitles, draft rubric-based feedback on short answers for teacher approval, summarize a recorded class. All teacher-in-the-loop, all included.

vs. Docebo at ~$40k/yr and Moodle, where AI needs plugins.
09

Self-hosting is a shipped product

One Go binary plus a compose file. A Gulf ministry or an EU university that requires data residency deploys in-region without an enterprise negotiation. Same code as cloud, licensed differently.

vs. Canvas / Docebo, where residency is a six-figure conversation.
10

Priced per active learner

Seat-based pricing punishes exactly the institutions you want — a 900-student madrasa with 200 monthly actives. Charge for use, publish the price, no "contact sales" on the entry tiers.

vs. TalentLMS at $119/mo for 40 seats and EzyCourse at $59–$299/mo — both unsellable at Dhaka or Lahore institutional price points.

Bidi and scripts — not i18n

To be precise about scope: the interface stays in English. No message catalogs, no translation workflow, no locale negotiation — that is a whole subsystem you do not need and would have to maintain forever. What you do need is that content renders correctly whatever script it arrives in, including mixed into English chrome. That is a much smaller, mostly one-time job, and it has to happen at the start.

Direction, per field
dir="auto" on every user-content element — the browser infers direction from the first strong character, per field, for free. An Arabic lesson title right-aligns itself inside an otherwise left-to-right admin table. Keep a per-course explicit override for the cases auto reads wrong: a title opening with a number, a date, or a Latin brand name.
Isolation, inline
Wrap any interpolated user string in <bdi>. This is where bidi bugs actually live: an Arabic name dropped into an English sentence drags the trailing punctuation to the wrong end. One wrapper component, applied once, and the class of bug never appears.
Logical properties only
margin-inline-start, padding-inline-end, inset-inline, text-align: start. Never left or right. A tenant-level dir="rtl" on the document root then flips the whole chrome for a Gulf customer with no extra CSS. Add a stylelint rule so nobody reintroduces a physical property in month six.
Fonts, subsetted
Noto Sans for Latin, Noto Sans Arabic, Noto Sans Bengali — self-hosted WOFF2 with unicode-range per face, so a Dhaka student on 3G downloads the Bengali file and never the Arabic one. Variable fonts where available; subset to the ranges you actually serve. Budget roughly 40–60 KB per script.
Vertical metrics
Bangla conjuncts and Arabic descenders clip at the line-height that looks right for Latin. Set line-height per script through a :lang() rule — around 1.5 for Latin, 1.75 for Bengali and Arabic — and never fix overflow by clipping.
PDF and image output
Certificates, transcripts and report cards need real text shaping. Go's PDF libraries generally do not shape, so render HTML through a headless Chromium worker and let the browser engine you already trust do the bidi and the ligatures. Slower per document, correct every time, and it reuses your existing templates.
Storage and comparison
Postgres in UTF-8 with ICU collations so Bangla and Arabic names sort the way a human expects. Normalize to NFC on write. Search uses the simple FTS config plus trigram for these scripts — the built-in stemmers do not cover them.
Numerals
Arabic-Indic digits (٠١٢٣) as a per-tenant display toggle. Store canonical Western digits always; convert only at render. Never parse a localized numeral back into a grade.

All of it is a few days of work at the start. Deferred, it becomes a rewrite of every stylesheet, every form input and every place a string is concatenated — which is why it sits in Phase 0 and not in a backlog.

Architecture

One Go binary, modular monolith, split into services only when a module measurably hurts. Microservices on day one would buy you nothing but deploy complexity and distributed bugs.

Contract
OpenAPI 3.1 spec is the source of truth. Generate Go server stubs and a TypeScript client from it. SvelteKit and Expo consume the same generated client — one contract, three consumers, no drift.
API
Go 1.24, stdlib net/http routing, chi if you want middleware sugar. Modules under internal/: identity, tenancy, catalog, enrollment, assessment, grading, billing, messaging, media, reporting. Cross-module calls go through interfaces, never direct table reads.
Data
Postgres 17. Shared schema, tenant_id on every row, row-level security enforced in the database — not in application code you'll forget once. sqlc for typed queries, goose for migrations.
Jobs
river — Postgres-backed queue. Video transcode callbacks, SMS sends, report generation, sync reconciliation. No Redis until you can prove you need it.
Media
Pluggable — see the next section. The core stores a media_asset row and asks a provider for a playback manifest. Ships with an embed provider; your transcoder drops in behind the same interface with no changes above it.
Storage
S3 API as the contract, MinIO or Garage self-hosted as the default backend. Any S3-compatible bucket works if a tenant would rather pay for one. Presigned direct uploads so bytes never touch the API process.
Live classes
Adapter over Jitsi Meet and BigBlueButton, both self-hosted and open source. BBB is the default for institutions — it has breakout rooms, polls and recording built in. Never write WebRTC yourself.
Realtime
Server-sent events for notifications and live-class presence. WebSockets only when something genuinely needs a duplex channel.
Search
Postgres full-text search, with per-language configs. Meilisearch when it stops being good enough — not before.
Auth
Own it. Phone-number-plus-OTP first (email is secondary in these markets), argon2id passwords, JWT access with rotating refresh tokens, device sessions. OIDC and SAML land in the corporate phase.
Residency
One regional deployment per data domain (Singapore, Bahrain, later Frankfurt), plus the single-tenant self-host build. Region is a deploy target, not a runtime branch.
Caching
In-process LRU for hot config and permission checks, plus correct HTTP cache headers and ETags so manifests and static assets never re-hit Go. Valkey only when a second node makes an in-process cache wrong.
Observability
OpenTelemetry from the first endpoint into Prometheus, Loki and Grafana, with GlitchTip for errors — the whole stack self-hosted and free. Structured slog, trace IDs on every response header. Retrofitting this after a production incident is how weekends die.
Deploy
One binary, Docker Compose, Caddy in front for automatic TLS and byte-range media serving. Scale by adding API containers behind Caddy; Postgres gets a bigger box and then read replicas. No Kubernetes until the org has someone whose job it is.

Media, made pluggable

Media is the only part of an LMS whose cost curve can outrun its revenue curve, so it gets its own boundary. The core never knows what a video is — it holds a media_asset row and asks a provider for something playable.

// internal/media/provider.go
type Provider interface {
    // Called after an upload or when a URL is pasted.
    Ingest(ctx context.Context, a *Asset, src Source) (State, error)
    // Called by the player. Returns an embed URL, an HLS manifest URL,
    // or a progressive file URL — expiring, scoped to one viewer.
    Playback(ctx context.Context, a *Asset, v Viewer) (Playback, error)
    // Provider tells the core what it can do; the UI adapts.
    Caps() Caps // renditions, captions, drm, offline, thumbnails
}

Four adapters, in the order they earn their place:

Embed phase 1
Paste a YouTube, Vimeo, Odysee, PeerTube or bare iframe URL; resolve via oEmbed; render in a sandboxed frame. Costs you nothing, ships in a day, and is how most BD and Gulf tutors already host video. Launch on this alone.
Direct file phase 1
Presigned upload to MinIO, served back through Caddy with byte-range support and an HMAC-signed expiring URL. No transcode — the file plays as uploaded. Good enough for audio, PDFs, slides and small video.
Your transcoder when ready
The interface is the integration point. You hand back a job ID on Ingest, hit a webhook when the ladder is built, and return an HLS manifest URL from Playback. Nothing above the interface changes. Until it exists, a stub adapter fronting ffmpeg in a River worker covers the gap.
Hosted optional
A Stream or Bunny adapter exists as a per-tenant escape hatch for a customer who wants to pay for it. Never the default, never a dependency.

The same shape applies everywhere a vendor could otherwise become load-bearing: PaymentProvider, LiveClassProvider, NotificationChannel, StorageBackend, AIProvider. One interface, a free default, a paid adapter only if someone asks.

The two rails you cannot self-host — say so honestly

Payment gateways (bKash, SSLCommerz, Tap, Stripe) and SMS / WhatsApp Business API are licensed rails, not tools. There is no open-source substitute; the adapter pattern is the mitigation — you are never locked to one, and a tenant can bring their own merchant account and SMS credentials so the cost sits on their side of the line, not yours.

On performance, the numbers that matter and where to hold them: API p95 under 150 ms — Go plus sqlc gets there if you never N+1 and index every foreign key. Under 100 KB on first paint — SvelteKit compiles away the framework, so this is achievable rather than aspirational. Media served by Caddy, never by Go — the API issues a signed URL and gets out of the way. Bandwidth metered per tenant from the first migration, because you cannot price what you did not measure.

Scale in the boring order: one box → separate Postgres → more API containers behind Caddy → read replicas for reporting → split a module out only when its metrics prove it. A single Go binary on a well-specified VM will carry tens of thousands of learners, and the four-figure monthly bill everyone else is paying is your margin.

Deliberately not building

Every one of these is cheaper to buy, and each would cost you a phase to build badly.

  • A transcoding pipeline, for now. Launch on the embed adapter. Your own transcoder lands behind the interface when it is ready — that ordering is what makes it a feature and not a blocker.
  • WebRTC. Jitsi, BBB or Zoom behind an adapter.
  • Microservices. Modular monolith until a module proves it needs to scale separately.
  • A plugin marketplace. Moodle's 2,000 plugins are also Moodle's biggest support cost. Webhooks and a clean API cover the real need.
  • Your own payment rails. Adapters over licensed gateways — the one place paying is unavoidable.
  • A page builder. Themeable templates until someone pays you to make it configurable.
  • Kubernetes. A Go binary on a VM with a load balancer will carry you to a revenue number worth celebrating.

What will actually hurt

Bidi retrofit
The single most expensive mistake available — it touches every stylesheet, every input and every string concatenation. Logical properties from the first rule, and a mixed-script screenshot test in CI.
Certificate PDFs
The one place bidi genuinely bites. Most Go PDF libraries do no text shaping, so Arabic renders as disconnected backwards letters and Bangla conjuncts fall apart — and nobody notices until a student downloads one.
Tenant leakage
One query missing a tenant filter ends the company. RLS in the database, plus an automated cross-tenant probe in the test suite.
Payment reconciliation
Local gateways have flaky webhooks and inconsistent idempotency. Every payment gets a state machine and a manual reconcile screen from day one.
Offline sync conflicts
Decide the rules before you write the code: quiz attempts are append-only, progress takes the max, submissions are last-write-wins with an audit trail.
Video cost
Egress is the bill that ends video products. The embed adapter defers it entirely at launch; when your transcoder arrives, cap renditions, cache hard at Caddy, and meter bandwidth per tenant — a column you add in the first migration, not the fortieth.
Self-hosting everything
Free software is not free to operate. MinIO, BBB, Postal and a Whisper GPU box are four things that can page you. Sequence them: nothing self-hosted lands before the phase that needs it, and each ships with a backup and a restore you have actually run.
Email deliverability
Self-hosted SMTP on a fresh IP goes straight to spam. Warm the IP, set SPF, DKIM and DMARC on day one, and keep a paid relay adapter ready — for these markets SMS and WhatsApp carry the important messages anyway.
EzyCourse already bundles a white-label app
On its $199+ tiers you get branded iOS and Android for free — so "we have a mobile app" is not a wedge in BD. Yours has to win on offline and low bandwidth, which theirs does not do.
The $60 script anchor
CodeCanyon LMS scripts sell once for under $100 and shape what a BD buyer thinks software costs. Do not compete there — sell the term running without a spreadsheet, and price per active learner so the number stays small.
Scope, three ways
Serving three buyer types is the strategy and also the risk. The rule: a feature lands in core only if two of the three need it. Otherwise it's a tenant-type module.
